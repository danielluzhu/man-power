MANPOWER

Carrier-pigeon messaging, except the courier is the fastest human alive.

Your message is not transmitted. It is carried — run across every landmass, swum across every sea, at world-record pace the whole way. It arrives when a human could have arrived, and not one second sooner.

      How a journey is timed

      1. 1

        Search for the fastest way

        Not the shortest — the soonest. A least-time search over a grid of the world, where every step costs whatever it costs to cross: flat ground, a mountainside, or open water.

      2. 2

        Let the cost do the deciding

        Swimming is three and a half times slower than running, so a route will go hundreds of kilometres out of its way to cross at a strait. Climbing costs time and thin air costs more, so it rounds mountain ranges rather than going over them. None of that is a rule — it all falls out of arriving soonest.

      3. 3

        Time each leg on its own record

        The chosen path is split where it meets a coast, and each leg timed against the world record for its distance — a six-hundred-metre river crossing swum at 800 m pace, the four thousand kilometres either side run at marathon pace, with gradient and altitude applied along the way.

      The pace book

      Records are applied as pace, not as a flat duration. A 400 m hop does not take Josh Kerr's full 3:42.66 — it takes 400 m at his mile pace. That is the only reading that stays coherent at the long end, where a marathon record has to cover thousands of kilometres. Between two records, pace is interpolated along the endurance curve.

      What the ground does to the pace

      Gradient uses Minetti's measurements of the metabolic cost of running on slopes, converted to a speed at constant effort — with the downhill bonus capped, because nobody actually runs a descent 1.67 times faster than the flat however much energy it saves. Altitude costs about 1% of performance per 100 m above 1500 m, which is what makes a high plateau something to go around rather than merely something long.

      Three records deliberately left out

      The 2 mile, 10 mile and 30 km bests are each slower in pace terms than their neighbours on the ladder — a sub-two-hour marathon outpaces the standing 30 km best. Including them would make the curve non-monotonic, which would let a longer journey arrive before a shorter one along the same route. They are left off, and the app refuses to start if the ladder ever stops falling.

      Run the whole thing

      The site above is only the calculator. The app itself is a small Bun server with SQLite behind it — handles, inboxes, sealed messages and a live globe tracking every courier in flight. 

      # clone and start
git clone https://github.com/danielluzhu/man-power.git
cd man-power && bun install
bun run server.js            # http://localhost:4321

# or install it as a systemd service
sudo deploy/install.sh       # restarts on failure, starts on boot

      You need at least two couriers for anything to happen — enlist a second one in a private window and write to yourself the long way round.
